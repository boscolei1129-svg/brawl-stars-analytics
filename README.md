# Brawl Stars Analytics

A stats tracker for Brawl Stars players and their clubs. It shows trophy growth, win rate, modes and maps, brawlers, the club member board, when club members play, and goals with ETAs. It started with player **#8YRJ090Q2** and club **#2U0RP22G2 (Stargazers)**; anyone can add another player.

## How it works

- **Welcome.** A device opening the app for the first time sees "hello" written out in one stroke, then a four-step tour: looking anyone up (Explore), tracking a player for history, goals and alerts, and how goals stay private to each device. "Start exploring" at the end opens Explore. Open the app with `?welcome` at the end of the link to see it again; "How it works" on the home screen replays the tour.
- **Explore first.** A device with no players saved opens on Explore, not the add form; **My stats** (or "Track a player" on Explore) leads to the home screen. Tracking is offered where it adds something: a "Want history?" card on each player, a line under "since you last looked" (Explore only sees two moments; tracking shows when each change happened), and a stronger card near the top when someone has opened the same player 3 times in a week or starred them. Visit times for this stay on the device for a week (up to 40 players).
- **Home screen.** From My stats, a device with no players sees the home screen: type a player tag, see the player (and their club) straight away, pick the club to follow (their own, another one, or none), and tap **Track for history**. No account or code is needed. After that the app opens straight to that player. Tap the player's name at the top to switch players or add another. Each device keeps its own list of players; removing one only takes it off that device.
- **Players.** Up to 50 players are tracked at once, and each device (with the devices linked to it) can start tracking 2 of them. Saving a player someone else already tracks doesn't count. Removing a player you started frees your spot; if no other device started them, they pause (history kept). A player nobody opens for 30 days pauses, and opening it again wakes it up while there's room. Anyone can view any tracked player (game stats are public). A player added with their own club follows them if they move to another; once a profile follows its player's own club, nobody can point it at a different one.
- **Explore.** A switch at the top of the app (My stats / Explore), and a "Just looking?" card on the home screen, open Explore: look up any player or club without tracking them. It has a player view (profile, brawlers, and a breakdown of the battles the game still lists, up to 25, with the time they cover), Head-to-head (two players side by side, per line, with no overall "winner"), Club scout (member spread, biggest share of the total, roster) and "since you last looked" (a small snapshot kept on the device). Links: `#explore`, `#explore/p/TAG`, `#explore/vs/TAG/TAG`, `#explore/club/TAG`. Looking someone up writes nothing about them or the visitor except a shared, short-lived copy of the public data (`explore_cache`: fresh for 2 minutes for players and 5 for clubs, kept up to a day as a fallback when the game's API is down) and hourly counts with no identifiers (`usage_counters`). Many people looking at the same player cost one call to the game; at most 1,000 fresh fetches an hour are made for everyone together, and each network can do 60 lookups every 10 minutes. Tracking stays a separate, explicit step.
- **The page** (`index.html` and `app.js`) is served by GitHub Pages. It reads saved stats from Supabase with the public, read-only key.
- **The server function** (`supabase/functions/brawl`) fetches fresh data from BrawlTime and saves a reading plus any new battles. The sync button calls it, and so does the app while it's open (every 3 minutes). It also keeps each device's goals and alert settings (see below) and limits how often any one address can call the expensive actions.
- **The schedule.** A Supabase cron job refreshes every player every 15 minutes (each one on its own minute), so history builds up even when the app is closed.
- **Old history.** Readings are saved when something changes, and most only hold the changes. Once a day is more than 30 days old, it is folded into its last reading (the day's changes added together), so it keeps the same end-of-day state in about 5% of the space. This runs hourly, a limited number of days at a time. Battles and play sessions are kept for a year, so trophy charts keep every battle.
- **Club data.** Club member lists, from BrawlTime and from Supercell's own API, can be up to about an hour behind the game. With an official Brawl Stars API key saved as the `BS_API_KEY` secret in Supabase, the function reads every member's live profile through RoyaleAPI's proxy (`bsproxy.royaleapi.dev`; allow IP `45.79.218.79` on the key) and adds up the club total itself. Without a key, only the player's own row is live.
- **Club activity.** Brawl Stars doesn't share who is online, so the app goes by play instead. The function reads each member's last 25 battles (at most every 4 minutes) and groups battles less than 20 minutes apart into play sessions. A member counts as recently active if one of their battles ended in the last 12 minutes. The Activity tab shows who has played recently, a 24-hour timeline for each of the last 7 days, the club's usual hours by weekday (a heatmap that fills in over two weeks), and the most active members this week. Tap a member for their sessions and usual hours. Times are in the viewer's own time zone.
- **Goals and alerts are private to each device.** Each device makes a random secret key the first time it opens the app and keeps it to itself; the server only stores a hash of it. Goals and alert settings belong to that key, so nobody else can see or change them, and there's nothing to guess or claim. To use them on another device (or on iPhone, in the Home Screen app, which keeps its own storage), tap **Use on another device** on the Goals tab for a one-time 6-digit code (10 minutes, single use, guesses limited per address and overall). Devices that used the old per-player codes hand them in once on their next open, which moves the goals made with them onto that device.
- **Notifications.** On the Goals tab, each device can turn on alerts for that player: goals reached, trophy milestones (every 500 for you, every 5,000 for the club), members joining or leaving, and a daily recap at a time it picks (in the device's own time zone). The recap covers the last 24 hours: your battles, trophies and win rate, plus the trophies the other members won for the club while in it, so new members joining (and your own trophies) don't count. On iPhone and iPad this works once the app is added to the Home Screen and opened from there. The server signs notifications with its own keys (made on first use, kept private in the database).
- **Opening fast.** The page, fonts, icons and game pictures stay on your device (`sw.js`), and so do the stats it has already downloaded. Pictures for the other tabs are fetched in the background after the app opens. An update downloads its complete page and script together, then activates after existing app tabs close.
- **Lean storage.** When a check finds nothing new, the server marks the last reading as confirmed instead of saving a copy. Brawler lists are saved as changes only.
- **Abuse limits.** The server counts calls per address (Supabase's gateway sets the caller's address, so it can't be faked; counters store only a salted hash of it and are deleted after two days) for player lookups (40 per 10 minutes), adding new players (6 per hour), new devices, link codes, notifications and the old code check (10 tries per player per hour). Over the limit, the app says to wait a few minutes. If the counter itself fails, anything that calls the game's API, creates a player or device, or lets someone guess a code is refused; goals, alerts and removing players carry on.
- **The database** (`supabase/schema.sql`) has these tables:
  - `profiles`: every tracked player and the club it follows.
  - `readings`: one row per update, per player.
  - `battles`: one row per battle, keyed by player and time.
  - `goals`: per player and per owner (a device, or devices linked together). Private.
  - `sessions`: club members' play sessions.
  - `profile_codes`: private. The old per-player codes (SHA-256), kept only so devices can hand them in once. To be dropped once no device still uses one.
  - `device_keys`, `device_links`: private. Hashes of each device's key and the owner it belongs to; one-time link codes (hashed).
  - `rate_hits`: private. Call counters for the abuse limits, cleared after two days.
  - `settings`: private. The current full brawler and club lists for each player, and the push keys.
  - `push_subs`: private. Devices that turned on notifications, per player.
  - `profile_adders`: private. Which device (owner) started tracking which player, for the 2-per-device limit. Spots are taken by `claim_slot`, one locked step, so two requests at the same moment can't both take the last spot.
  - `explore_cache`: private. Short-lived copies of public player and club data fetched by Explore.
  - `usage_counters`: private. Hourly counts of lookups, cache hits, fresh fetches, game API errors and limits hit, and of players tracked by where the add started (`track:new|save|wake:explore|home`). No identifiers; kept 90 days.

  Anyone can read `profiles`, `readings`, `battles` and `sessions` (public game stats). The public key can't write to any table; only the function can, and only it can read the rest.

## Privacy

The app has no accounts and collects no names, emails, locations, ads or analytics. What it stores is listed under **The database** above, and in plain words in the app (**Privacy and data** on the home screen, or **About and privacy** at the bottom of the Me tab). Player stats are public game data; goals and alert settings are private to the device that made them.

## Add to your home screen

- **iPhone:** open the site in Safari, tap Share, then tap **Add to Home Screen**.
- **Android:** open the site in Chrome, open the menu, then tap **Add to Home screen**.

## Notes

- Each player's trophy history starts when it was added (Oct 19, 2026 for #8YRJ090Q2). Club activity starts on Oct 20, 2026; days before that only include each member's last 25 battles.
- Solo Showdown counts a top-5 finish as a win, as BrawlTime does.
- A free Supabase project can pause after a week without activity. The cron job's calls should keep it awake. If it ever pauses, restore it from the Supabase dashboard.
- Fonts: Chakra Petch and JetBrains Mono, both under the SIL Open Font License (see `fonts/`). The welcome "hello" is traced from the letterforms of Dancing Script (SIL Open Font License).
- Game art is loaded from Brawlify and BrawlTime. This app is unofficial and not affiliated with, endorsed, sponsored or approved by Supercell; see Supercell's Fan Content Policy.
- Club activity is an estimate: the game doesn't share who is online, so the app shows members as **recently active** when one of their battles ended in the last 12 minutes, never as "online".

## Tests

See [DEPLOYMENT.md](DEPLOYMENT.md) for all audit regression tests, the disposable PostgreSQL concurrency test, browser checks and the required backend/frontend release order. The framing response header still requires hosting support.

`tests/fn_security.js` runs the real server function in Node against an in-memory database and checks that one device can't see, change or delete another device's goals, that link codes work once and expire, that guesses and expensive calls are limited, and that goal alerts only reach the goal's owner: `npm i esbuild && node tests/fn_security.js supabase/functions/brawl/index.ts`. `tests/fn_limits.js` checks the player limits (50 in the app, 2 started per device, releasing, pausing and waking) and that thinning old readings gives exactly the same end-of-day state: `node tests/fn_limits.js supabase/functions/brawl/index.ts`. `tests/fn_explore.js` checks Explore: nothing is tracked, the shared cache and request joining (6 lookups of one player make one fetch), not-found caching, stale copies when the game's API is down, the per-network and app-wide limits, what happens when the limit counter itself fails (lookups, adding players and new devices are refused; goals keep working), and that two devices racing for the last spot can't both get it: `node tests/fn_explore.js supabase/functions/brawl/index.ts`.
